When Reasons Aren't Enough

Kiddo...

I think one of the most frustrating things about this post is that you seem to feel as though everyone is responding to something different from what you're actually trying to communicate...

You tell your psychiatrist something deeply serious.. And suddenly the conversation shifts towards admission.. Your dad wants to introduce a schedule... You're reminded of how fortunate you are and how much has already been prepared for your future...

And somewhere in all of that.. The thing you're trying to explain seems to get lost...

I want to take each part of this separately...

First.. Your psychiatrist appointment...

You said you wish you had kept your mouth shut and never told him what had been happening over the past couple of weeks...

I can understand why you're having that reaction... You were honest about something you probably found incredibly difficult to say.. And now you're facing consequences that feel frightening and completely outside your control...

Perhaps you expected to be able to tell him what was happening without your entire situation changing afterwards... Instead.. You've been confronted with the possibility of admission.. And now you're anticipating your father's reaction too...

But I want you to consider something...

The fact that your psychiatrist is recommending admission doesn't mean you made a mistake by telling him... It means that what you described was serious enough to require a different level of care...

I know that's probably not what you wanted to hear... I know you might feel exposed.. Embarrassed and even regretful... But I don't want you to confuse the discomfort of facing the consequences with the idea that you should have remained silent...

Please don't take back your honesty simply because the truth has made things more complicated...

If you're frightened about admission.. Ask your psychiatrist to explain exactly what it would involve... Ask what your concerns and preferences would mean in practice... You deserve to understand what's happening rather than being left to imagine the worst...

And please work with him on the recommendation... You can disagree with aspects of the approach and still recognise that you need care right now.

Then there's your dad...

You said you appreciate everything he does.. But you just want to be left alone...

I think there's an important distinction here...

You aren't necessarily rejecting his efforts... You're frustrated by the way those efforts are being translated into expectations placed on you...

I imagine that having someone suddenly become heavily involved in your daily life.. Especially when you're already struggling to manage it yourself.. Can feel like losing whatever little control you still have...

And that brings me to the schedule...

I noticed that you were quite specific about this... You said you already have a routine.. And that you're comfortable adding to it or changing it yourself... What you dislike is being given a schedule...

I think what you're trying to preserve is a sense of autonomy...

A routine can feel like something that belongs to you... You know its general shape.. You can adjust it when necessary.. And you aren't necessarily accountable to someone else for every part of it...

A schedule imposed on you might feel entirely different... It can turn ordinary activities into obligations.. And every deviation can start feeling like another failure...

I don't think it's unreasonable to want some control over your own day...

However.. I would encourage you to distinguish between the structure itself and the way it's being introduced...

Perhaps you could sit down with your dad and explain that you're more willing to work with a flexible routine than a rigid timetable... You could agree on a few important daily priorities together.. While allowing yourself some freedom over when and how you approach them...

You could even ask him to let you have a say before introducing changes...

That way.. You're not simply being handed a list of expectations... You're participating in creating something you can realistically work with...

I don't think the answer has to be either complete freedom or someone else controlling every hour of your day...

There can be a middle ground...

Now.. About what your dad said regarding how fortunate you are...

You seem to understand exactly what he's trying to tell you... You recognise the opportunities you've been given.. The security your family has provided and the future that your dad and grandpa have worked to make possible...

You aren't pretending those things don't exist...

But I think the problem is that being reminded of them doesn't address what you're actually feeling...

You already know you're fortunate... You already know that you have options... You already know that there are things ahead of you that other people might dream of having...

And yet.. You still feel hopeless...

I wonder whether that leaves you feeling even more frustrated with yourself... Almost as though you should be able to appreciate your circumstances enough to feel motivated.. But somehow you can't make that happen...

I want you to understand that gratitude and emotional wellbeing aren't interchangeable...

You can be genuinely grateful for everything your family has done and still feel deeply unhappy... You can recognise that your future is full of possibilities without feeling any excitement about reaching it...

Being shown how much you have doesn't necessarily make you feel capable of enjoying it...

And I don't think you should be expected to use your privileges as an argument against your own feelings...

Your dad may be trying to remind you of what you have to look forward to... I can understand that intention... But perhaps what you're trying to communicate is that knowing there's a future waiting for you doesn't automatically give you the desire to move towards it...

Those are two different conversations.. And I hope he eventually understands the distinction...

Then you explain what you believe the actual problem is...

You say you don't think you're stupid... You don't dislike everything about your life... You don't necessarily believe you have no future...

You've simply lost motivation...

I think it's worth looking more closely at that statement...

Because you aren't describing a lack of opportunities or an inability to recognise good things... You're describing a disconnect between knowing that those things exist and actually feeling moved to pursue them...

It's as though you can look at your life and identify things that should matter to you.. But that recognition doesn't translate into the desire to get up and participate in it...

And I imagine that can be incredibly difficult to explain to someone who keeps offering you reasons to be motivated...

They might think that if they remind you of your future... Your family.. Your friends or your opportunities.. You'll eventually find something that makes everything click...

But you already know those things...

The difficulty isn't necessarily that you can't identify a reason... It's that identifying one doesn't make you feel any different...

I also noticed the sentence about accepting the difficult days.. Even if you get more scars each time.

I don't want to overlook that...

You seem to have reached a point where the difficult days have become something you expect to happen.. Something you have learned to accommodate rather than something you believe will change...

But I don't want you to mistake becoming accustomed to something for it becoming manageable...

You shouldn't have to keep absorbing more pain simply because you've learned how to carry it...

And then you mention the little things that still make you smile...

I found this part interesting because you don't seem to be denying that happiness exists in your life...

You mention this person you like.. The way he makes you smile whenever you text him.. Your friends being patient and understanding.. Joking with your mum and spending time with your sister.

I imagine those moments feel quite different from the rest of what you've been describing...

There's something uncomplicated about enjoying a conversation with whoever that you said you like talking to. You aren't necessarily thinking about your future.. Your responsibilities or everything you haven't managed to do... You're simply enjoying the interaction...

And I think that's worth appreciating... So whoever that dude is talk to him... And me or anyone if they make you happy...

I hope you don't start treating those moments as insignificant just because they don't solve everything else...

If someone makes you smile.. Enjoy talking to them... If your friends make you feel understood.. Appreciate their company... If spending time with your sister makes you happy.. Let yourself enjoy it...

You don't have to turn those moments into something more than they are...

But I think the next part is where you're being particularly hard on yourself...

You ask.. Essentially.. Whether those moments should be enough reason to get up every day...

And your answer is that they aren't...

I don't think you're saying those people or experiences mean nothing to you... You're saying that their existence hasn't restored your desire to keep going...

There's a difference...

You can genuinely look forward to a conversation and still struggle with the thought of another day... You can enjoy someone's company without suddenly feeling motivated about everything else...

One happy moment doesn't necessarily cancel out everything that follows it...

And I don't want you to feel that you have to make yourself appreciate those moments more intensely until they somehow become sufficient...

They are allowed to be small moments of happiness... They don't have to carry the responsibility of keeping your entire world together...

I'm glad there are people who bring you happiness.

Enjoy those connections for what they are... Let them be part of your life...

And then there's the guilt...

You call yourself selfish for feeling this way despite having people and experiences that make you happy...

I think you're judging yourself for something you haven't chosen...

You didn't decide that those moments would stop feeling sufficient... You didn't consciously choose to lose your motivation... And you aren't deliberately refusing to appreciate the people around you...

You're describing an emotional state that you yourself seem unable to reconcile...

Please don't turn that confusion into another accusation against yourself...

You can be grateful for your friends.. Love your family.. Enjoy talking to someone and still be struggling... You don't have to feel guilty for the difference between what you recognise intellectually and what you're actually experiencing emotionally...

But.. Kiddo...

When you say you've given up.. That you have no will to live.. And that fear of dying is the only thing keeping you alive..

Those are deeply concerning words.. Especially alongside what you told your psychiatrist about the past couple of weeks...

I want to return to something you said earlier.

You mentioned that your dad and grandpa have prepared a future for you.. Ready for you to choose...

I hope that.. Eventually.. You get to choose that future because you genuinely want to explore it...

Not because someone has prepared it for you... Not because you feel obligated to make use of everything you've been given... Not because you think you owe anyone a particular kind of life...

I hope you find interests that excite you.. Ambitions that feel like your own and experiences that make you curious about what's coming next...

You don't need to have all of that figured out right now...

And you certainly don't need to manufacture enthusiasm just to satisfy the people around you...

For now.. I want you to be honest about how little motivation you have.. How hopeless you feel and how seriously those feelings have affected your desire to live...

You don't need to dress any of it up as something more acceptable...

I hope your dad can eventually understand that what you're asking for isn't simply another list of reasons to be grateful or another schedule to follow... You're trying to communicate something that goes much deeper than either of those things...

And I hope you can allow yourself to be heard without feeling that you have to defend every emotion you experience.

I love you.. Kddo...

I hope the little things that make you smile continue to find their way into your days... I hope conversations become something you look forward to... That you get to enjoy your friendships without feeling guilty about them.. And that the future eventually becomes something you feel curious about rather than something that merely exists in front of you...

But above all.. Please give yourself the chance to experience that future...

You don't have to feel hopeful today to deserve the possibility of being happy tomorrow...

End of letter.
