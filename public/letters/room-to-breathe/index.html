Kiddo...

I think there's something we need to separate here... Your family's concern for you.. And the way they're expressing that concern...

Because those aren't necessarily the same thing...

I don't think your parents are wrong for wanting you to eat.. Shower.. Pray or get out of the house... Those are important things.. And I imagine they're worried when they see you struggling with them... But I also don't think repeatedly reminding you to do those things is necessarily helping...

There's a difference between encouraging someone and making them feel as though they're being constantly supervised... And I think that's where a lot of your frustration is coming from...

When someone keeps telling you to do something.. Even after you've heard them the first time.. It can start feeling less like a reminder and more like an instruction you're expected to obey... And eventually the actual task becomes associated with the frustration of being told to do it...

I think you should try explaining that to your parents when things are calm... But I'd suggest being specific about what you want them to do differently...

For example.. You could tell them that you'd rather they ask you once instead of repeatedly reminding you... That you'd appreciate being given some time to do things at your own pace.. And that if you need help.. You'll try to communicate that instead of shutting everyone out...

But I also want you to meet them halfway here...

If you tell them you'll eat later.. Try to follow through... If you say you'll shower in the evening.. Make an effort to do it... Not because you need to prove something to them.. But because trust works both ways... The more they can see that you're taking responsibility for certain things yourself.. The easier it may become for them to give you the space you're asking for...

And I think the lunch situation is worth addressing separately...

You weren't particularly hungry earlier because you'd had a late breakfast.. You planned to eat later.. And when you finally went to get the pizza your mother had saved for you.. Someone had already eaten it... So you were left hungry and frustrated without having the energy to prepare something else...

I can understand why that annoyed you...

But I want you to think about how you could prevent a similar situation next time... Perhaps you could ask your mother to keep your portion somewhere separate.. Or agree on an alternative in case the food you were expecting isn't available... It might also help to have a couple of simple meals you can put together yourself when you don't have much energy...

Nothing complicated.. Just something you can manage without having to make an entire meal from scratch...

I know that might sound like a small thing.. But sometimes having a practical solution to a recurring problem can save you from unnecessary arguments...

And please don't let one frustrating situation turn into an entire day of not eating... You deserve to have your basic needs taken care of even when you're upset...

Now.. About your mother...

I don't think you need to keep score of who gets angry more often... That's unlikely to lead to anything productive... But I do think you should be able to tell her when something she's said has hurt you...

When she said.. "Is this how someone who's trying to get better acts?".. I imagine it felt as though everything you'd managed to do that day had suddenly been dismissed because of one difficult moment...

And that's something you could explain to her...

You could tell her that you understand she gets frustrated too.. But when she questions whether you're even trying.. It makes you feel as though your efforts don't count unless they're visible to her...

You don't have to accuse her of being a bad mother or turn it into an argument about her temper... Just explain what those comments do to you and what you'd appreciate instead...

And remember.. You can love your mother without agreeing with everything she says... You can understand that she's worried and still be upset with the way she handles certain situations... Neither feeling cancels out the other...

I also want to talk about the things you did that day...

You mentioned showering.. Going out the previous night and helping at the shop because you didn't want to be alone...

I think you should continue looking for opportunities like that...

Not because you need to keep yourself occupied every second of the day.. And certainly not because you need to turn every ordinary activity into some sort of achievement... But because having somewhere to go.. Something to do or someone to interact with can give your day a little direction...

And I found the shop part particularly nice...

You weren't particularly close to the guy working there.. Yet he welcomed you helping out.. Didn't interrogate you about why you were suddenly there and simply allowed you to be part of what was happening...

I think you should remember that experience...

You don't always need a deep conversation or someone asking you how you're feeling... Sometimes it's enough to be around people.. Have something to occupy yourself with and feel included in an ordinary part of someone else's day...

Perhaps you could visit the shop again.. Help out when it's appropriate or find other small activities where you can interact with people without feeling like you're being examined...

And if you enjoy that kind of company.. Don't be afraid to seek it out...

You don't have to isolate yourself just because you don't feel like explaining what's going on in your life...

Now.. About your cousins' place...

I think you should genuinely consider what made being there easier for you...

You mentioned that your aunt and grandparents still checked on you... They still asked whether you'd eaten or prayed.. But they weren't as persistent.. And they didn't turn every reminder into a source of frustration...

Perhaps what you appreciated most was that they trusted you to respond in your own time...

I think that's an important distinction.. Because it gives you something concrete to communicate to your parents...

Instead of simply telling them that you want to be left alone.. Try explaining what you experienced at your cousins' place that you would like to have at home too...

Maybe you could tell them that you don't mind being checked on.. But you need them to trust you enough to give you time to act on those reminders... Maybe you could ask for more ordinary conversations.. More shared activities and fewer conversations that revolve around what you haven't done...

And if you genuinely feel that staying with your cousins again would help.. I think you should discuss that possibility with your family...

You don't have to dismiss the idea simply because you had an argument at home... But please don't run away or leave without telling anyone... If you want to stay there for a while.. Try to arrange it properly with your family and your relatives... Explain why you think the change of environment would help and what kind of arrangement you're hoping for...

You deserve to have a say in where you stay.. But I want you making that decision calmly and safely rather than in the middle of a particularly difficult day...

And finally.. Your psychiatrist...

I want you to be honest with yourself about this part...

You say you don't want to go back because it doesn't help.. You don't feel the medication is working.. And the increased involvement from your parents has made you feel even more restricted...

I won't pretend that those frustrations are insignificant... If you feel that your treatment isn't helping.. You should be able to say so without feeling as though you're being difficult...

But I don't want you to make a permanent decision about your treatment based on how frustrated you feel right now...

Instead.. Tell your psychiatrist exactly what's bothering you... Tell him what you feel hasn't improved.. What you dislike about the medication and how the situation at home has been affecting you... Ask him what can be changed or reassessed and what other approaches might be available...

And please don't stop or change your medication without speaking to the person prescribing it...

You should also be able to tell him that you want more involvement in decisions about your own care... That you want your parents to support you without making you feel as though every part of your day is being monitored...

I think it's important that you understand this... Receiving help doesn't mean you lose your right to have opinions about that help... You can question things.. Express discomfort and ask for a different approach while still allowing yourself to receive the care you need...

You don't have to pretend everything is working perfectly just to make other people comfortable...

But please don't shut the door on the possibility of things improving either...

And there's one last thing I want to mention...

I think you and your parents might be approaching the same situation from two very different directions...

You want them to trust you enough to give you space... They might be worried that giving you that space means they're neglecting you or failing to help... So they keep reminding you.. You feel increasingly pressured.. They see you withdrawing further and become even more persistent...

And then the cycle repeats...

I think the way out of that cycle is going to require some compromise from both sides...

You could agree on a few things that you'll take responsibility for yourself.. While asking your parents to give you more freedom over when and how you do them... You could also agree on certain times when they'll check in rather than having reminders throughout the day...

Start small... Don't promise to change everything overnight... And don't agree to a routine that you know isn't realistic just because you want the conversation to end...

Give yourself something manageable to work with...

And if you have a difficult day.. Try not to treat that as proof that everything you've been working towards has fallen apart... One frustrating afternoon doesn't erase the effort you made earlier that day...

I suppose what I'm trying to say is that you don't have to choose between being constantly monitored and having to manage absolutely everything alone...

You can want independence without rejecting support... You can ask for patience without expecting everyone to understand you immediately... And you can take responsibility for yourself without pretending that you never need anyone's help...

I hope your parents eventually understand that giving you some room to breathe doesn't mean they care any less about you...

And I hope you learn to communicate what you need without feeling that you have to reach your breaking point before anyone listens...

Take things one step at a time.. Kiddo...

End of letter.
